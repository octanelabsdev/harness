    A story¶

    For one feature or one bug with clear edges: something you could describe in a paragraph and finish in a day or two. Bigger than that, or several pieces that only work together: split it into stories, one per piece you could ship on its own.

    Example: Trailhead shows the wrong tax total¶

    A customer reports that a two-night booking shows tax for one night.

    1. Decide (skip if you already know)¶

    You: What should I work on next?

    product-manager reads the board's real signals (pinned, blocking, priority, age) and proposes the top item. Here it's the tax bug, because it's pinned and two other cards wait on it.

    2. Write it up¶

    You: Write this up as a story.

    story-writer turns the report into a Definition-of-Ready card: what's in, what's out, acceptance criteria. Where the report is silent, it asks instead of guessing:

    Claude: Is tax charged per night or per booking? The report doesn't say, and the code does both in different places.

    You: Per night, at the rate on the booking date.

    Answer these yourself. An open question answered by a guess is how the wrong thing gets built correctly. The card passes a deterministic lint and is recorded at .claude/current-story.yaml, which is what unlocks the pipeline gate.

    3. Build¶

    You: Build it.

    Claude (through your agents, if the project has them) writes a failing test for the reported case, then the fix. When the turn ends, the Stop gate runs your tests. Red keeps the turn going:

    VERIFICATION GATE — test_command failed: bin/rails test
BookingTest#test_two_night_tax: expected 24.0, got 12.0

    Your job here is light: glance at the diff, answer questions.

    4. Review¶

    You: Blind-review this.

    The reviewer gets the diff and the project's rules, but not the story or the conversation, so it judges the code on its own terms. Critical findings go back for a fix, then it reviews again. With review_gate on, a turn can't end on a stale or failing review, and any edit after the review makes it stale.

    5. Commit¶

    Tests green and review passed: commit. Committing doesn't make the review stale; editing does.

    Signs it's bigger than one story¶

    • The story-writer keeps finding open questions that need someone else's answer.
    • One story turns into "and then we also need…" three times.

    Split it: one story per piece you could ship on its own, with the open questions settled before you write the next one.
